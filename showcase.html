01 — Overview

A rainforest that generates itself.

Amazon Expedition 3D is a complete, playable 3D world shipped as one HTML file and one library. The world is not modelled — it is computed. A closed-form height function carves rolling hills, cuts a river channel through the middle of the map, and raises a temple mound at the far end. Everything that stands on that surface is scattered by seeded random placement, then animated by parametric trigonometry.

The design brief was simple: maximum atmosphere for minimum bytes. No textures, no mesh files, no audio files — every surface is shaded by a Lambert or Phong material, every creature is eight lines of primitive geometry, and every sound is a synthesised oscillator. What the player gets is a jungle that feels alive because the maths says it should.

Zero-install delivery

Open the file. No server, no npm, no build pipeline. The Three.js runtime is vendored next to it and the whole experience is served by the browser's own engine.

Deterministic systems

Height, river depth, ruin placement, wildlife orbits and pickups are all closed-form functions — inspectable, tunable and readable, which is what makes a 747-line project still feel architected.

One-hour loop, real tension

Energy drains as you sprint and wade, restores when you rest, and forces route decisions: cross the river fast and pay for it, or take the long jungle path.

02 — Identity

A logo system, not a logo.

The mark was drawn as vectors for this project: a tapered triangle reading as both a jungle canopy and the letter A, a cyan river bar cutting through its crossbar, a gold gem crowning the apex, and a hill line grounding it. The same construction scales from a 290 px badge down to a 24 px favicon without redrawing.

04 — Systems

Five systems, one file.

The world is only convincing because five small systems hold hands. Each is self-contained, each is readable, and each can be replaced without touching the others.

Terrain & worldgen

    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        01
        
    
        
    Analytic heightfield
    
        
    Three stacked sine fields produce the hills, a windowed falloff carves the river, and a radial ramp lifts the temple mound — one pure function, called for terrain build, object placement and player grounding alike.
    
      
    
      
    
        02
        
    
        
    Closed-form wildlife
    
        
    No AI, no pathfinding, no state machines. Each creature is a parametric orbit with a phase, a radius and a speed — and a butterfly lands on the terrain because it asks the same height function the ground does.
    
      
    
      
    
        03
        
    
        
    Survival economy
    
        
    Energy is a single scalar with two drains (sprint, wading) and one restore (rest). It is the only resource in the game, which makes every route a legible trade-off instead of a puzzle.
    
      
    
      
    
        04
        
    
        
    Canvas-2D radar
    
        
    A second, entirely separate renderer draws the minimap: river band, tree scatter, live pickups and a rotating player arrow. Different API, same source of truth, refreshed every frame.
    
      
    
      
    
        05
        
    
        
    Synthesised feedback
    
        
    Every sound is an oscillator with an exponential gain ramp: 880 Hz rises to 1320 Hz on a gem, a three-note figure plays on victory. Zero bytes of audio, instant latency.
    
      
    
      
    
        06
        
    
        
    Light & atmosphere
    
        
    Hemisphere fill plus one directional key at 2048² soft shadows, exponential fog tinted to the canopy, and a bobbing camera that breathes with the player's stride.

The stack on the right is separated by distance, not by borders: the player is on the bottom plate, the world generation above it, the renderer above that, and the interface floating highest — exactly the order in which a frame is built.       07 — Roadmap
      
    Where the jungle goes next.
    
      
    v1.0 is done and playable. The next passes deepen the systems that already exist rather than bolting on new ones.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    v1.0 — First light Shipped
    
        
    Full jungle, river crossing, temple ruins, Golden Idol quest, 10 gems, energy system, wildlife, minimap, victory state. Playable end to end in a single HTML file.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    v1.1 — Weather & time of day Next
    
        
    A sun-and-moon cycle driving the directional light and sky colour, plus a rain state that increases wading drain and populates the canopy. The lighting rig is already built for it.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    v1.2 — Camp & inventory Next
    
        
    Placeable campfires that restore energy faster, and a small inventory so gems become tradeable rather than purely score-bearing. Both reuse the existing collider and pickup systems.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    v1.3 — Wildlife depth Planned
    
        
    Replace fixed orbits with steering behaviours, add riverbank fish and night insects, and let monkeys react to the player when the idol leaves its pedestal.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    v2.0 — WebGPU & co-op Speculative
    
        
    Migrate the renderer to a modern pipeline for instanced foliage and cascaded shadows, then open the world to two-player local co-op. This is an ambition, not a commitment.
